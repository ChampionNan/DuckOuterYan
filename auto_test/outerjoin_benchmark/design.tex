\subsection{Outer-Join Benchmark}
\label{sec:outerjoin-benchmark}

Standard analytical benchmarks do not isolate the factors that make outer-join
optimization difficult. TPC-H~\cite{tpch}, TPC-DS~\cite{tpcds}, JOB~\cite{job},
and SQLStorm~\cite{sqlstorm25} provide realistic schemas and join relationships,
but they do not systematically vary preservation semantics and join-tree shape.
We therefore use their queries as sources of realistic \emph{join skeletons},
rather than treating an inner-join query and its outer-join rewrite as
semantically equivalent workloads.

The core benchmark contains 34 traceable seeds: 5 from TPC-H, 7 from TPC-DS,
10 from JOB, and 12 from SQLStorm. From each seed, we extract a connected,
acyclic equality-join skeleton containing 3--6 relation instances. We retain
the relation graph, normalize every active edge to one plain column equality,
label primary/control joins FULL OUTER, and retain a mixed alternate only when
a bushy grouping is unavailable. Each generated selection remains in the global \texttt{WHERE}
clause and is NULL-rejecting. Unary \texttt{ON} conjuncts and equality-side
casts are excluded because they are outside OuterYan's current applicability
envelope. The FULL primary/control labels let filter pushdown simplify
preservation direction without reducing the complete graph to a pure inner query.

Each skeleton produces five deterministic applicability-oriented groupings: primary
left- and right-deep trees (\texttt{ldmx}/\texttt{rdmx}); a balanced bushy tree
when the graph permits one, otherwise an applicability-checked mixed left-deep traversal
(\texttt{bmx}/\texttt{alt}); and alternate left- and right-deep traversals that
start from the opposite graph side (the legacy filename slots
\texttt{lduX}/\texttt{rduX}). This gives
170 queries with 80 left-deep, 68 right-deep, and 22 genuinely bushy trees.
Explicit parentheses fix the binary join tree.

This construction provides a controlled and representative measure of
outer-join performance. Holding the relation graph fixed while varying tree
grouping isolates enumeration behavior from unrelated query-content changes.
The left-deep, right-deep, and bushy trees span multiple source schemas, while
the applicability-oriented labels ensure that every generated case reaches
OuterYan rather than being discarded after pre-optimizer simplification. Projecting columns from
multiple inputs keeps NULL-extension observable whenever it survives the
declared NULL-rejecting selection.

We also include the legacy workload in
\path{auto_test/outerjoin_query_old/} as a separate industry-oriented test
corpus. Its 170 outer-join queries are derived from workload patterns observed
in DBMS-X and instantiated over public benchmark schemas, thereby simulating
real industrial use cases while remaining reproducible. The corpus covers
practical patterns such as optional dimension enrichment, sparse relationships,
fact-completeness checks, and preserved master data, complementing the
controlled variants in the core benchmark.
